- Target: `u20-Z790-S-DDR4`, 2026-10-30 approximately 22:30 Asia/Shanghai. Reapplied the already deployed helper from commit `83043ee06abf2cc357ce86ac7ff58ea628655d5b`, verified on the host as SHA-256 `f21d51f036ae052b675327cc5fc81b26742d7dceb87f1a34a7574555ef504fe9`. No code, unit, configuration or Slurm controller was replaced.
- Observation before action: `/dev/nvidia0`, `/dev/nvidiactl` and `/dev/nvidia-uvm` had `other::rw-`, although the earlier release had removed that access. The cause and exact time of that change are not established; earlier isolated evidence showed that `ub-device-create` can reset node permissions, but does not prove it caused this particular event.
- With the Slurm queue empty, the root-owned helper was run directly. Backup of the immediately preceding ACLs, including `/dev/nvidia-uvm-tools`, is `/var/backups/time-gpu-acl-reapply.6QaFRAhQ/devices.before.acl`. Afterward, all three observed device ACLs showed `other::---`, the training-group denial, the runtime-group grant, and named administrator and `gdm` access.
- Bounded real opens of `/dev/nvidia0` were denied for `nobody` and `timzhang` and allowed for `u20`. `slurmctld`, `slurmd`, and the training backend remained active; the queue was empty, and the existing Sunshine PID continued to hold 384 MiB. No real training job or display service was stopped or restarted. These checks do not establish every user's access, scheduler launch behavior, or persistence across device recreation.
- Rollback was not needed or performed. The backup permits restoration via `setfacl --restore` after checking the saved paths and device identities, but would reinstate the observed world access and should not be applied blindly. This reapplication is a point-in-time mitigation; no durable, race-free device creation protection has been established. Monthly quota enforcement remains disabled.

## Immediate recurrence

At approximately 22:31 the same device again showed `other::rw-`. A bounded sequence on the host reapplied the helper and observed `other::---`, then invoked `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=index --format=csv,noheader`, after which `other::rw-` was observed. The helper was reapplied once more. This establishes recurrence in that sequence, not which concurrent process performed the permission change. The earlier direct-open results above applied only before that recurrence and must not be read as durable isolation. The backend samples GPU information periodically; do not assume reapplying the helper alone prevents bypass, or enable the monthly gate as a complete enforcement solution on that basis. Further root-cause investigation and a race-free enforcement design are required.

An additional root-run `strace -f` of that exact `nvidia-smi` command captured the process itself calling `chmod` with `0666` on `/dev/nvidiactl`, `/dev/nvidia0`, `/dev/nvidia-uvm`, and `/dev/nvidia-uvm-tools`. `/proc/driver/nvidia/params` reported `ModifyDeviceFiles: 1`, `DeviceFileUID: 0`, `DeviceFileGID: 0`, and `DeviceFileMode: 438` (octal `0666`). The helper was reapplied after the trace and removed the current `other` permission; a later 7-second observation still showed `other::---`. This does not prove persistence: any subsequent privileged invocation of the observed NVIDIA tool may reopen access. No driver parameters or modules were changed, and changing them has not yet been validated for live desktop and scheduled jobs.
