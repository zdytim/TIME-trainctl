This is source/config evidence, not a runtime bypass proof.

At observation, `timzhang` was a member of `pro6000d-training` and not `pro6000d-gpu`. NVIDIA compute nodes had ACL entries `group:pro6000d-training:---`, `group:pro6000d-gpu:rw-`, and `user:u20:rw-`, but `other::rw-` remained. The observed platform broker submits jobs with `--uid` for the user and `--gid` for `pro6000d-gpu`. Thus enrolled non-admin user access relies on POSIX ACL group matching, while the job's requested primary group is intended to allow allocated access under cgroup constraints. `u20` is an explicitly allowed administrator. A non-enrolled local account can fall through to world-readable/writable GPU device permissions; newly created accounts must not be assumed restricted. Other ACL group memberships, process credentials, device recreation, active sessions and privileged access affect the actual boundary.

Both ACL restoration and loopback firewall services were active. No access experiment, training job, ACL mutation or service restart was performed. These observations do not establish that scheduled PyTorch access works, that every route is blocked, or that monthly GPU-hour enforcement is active. Before enabling the new gate, review enrolled identities, device rules and a safe non-disruptive enforcement validation plan. Do not broadly remove device access without identifying desktop/system consumers and protecting live tasks.
