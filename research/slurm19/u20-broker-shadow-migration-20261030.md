The baseline passed SQLite integrity checking; the candidate initialized its additive schema on the shadow copy; and the old backend then reopened that copy. Schema and content digests for all eight existing application tables matched the baseline: five jobs, one application, seven notifications, twelve audit entries, one quota override and no other rows. The final copy passed SQLite integrity checking. Counts and digests describe the snapshot at that moment only; the checker did not exercise job submission, rendering, authentication or a real service restart. The live database was opened read-only, and no production service, Slurm controller, code, config or job was changed. The private snapshots contain operational data and must not be committed or copied into public artifacts.

The checker subsequently disables bytecode creation to avoid that cleanup issue. This is not a production migration or rollback and does not establish WAL power-loss recovery or monthly gate readiness.
